Run sheet

Seventeen slides, six blocks

Slide by slide: what is on screen, what we say, and what the room does. Two hours fifty with a break. Times are cumulative from the start.

Blue rows: we present Green rows: they work in the workbook Grey rows: break or questions
0:00 Slide 1 What the DPDP Act asks of you, and when 3 min

Say: Most of this Act is not in force. The part that reaches you lands on 13 May 2027, and the work it implies takes longer than the time left. Then read the four things they leave holding.

Do: Give the shape of the session, seven blocks, present then work then questions. Ask them to open the workbook link in a second tab and leave it there. Say plainly that nothing they type reaches us.

Block 1 · When this reaches you · 0:03 to 0:20

0:03 Slide 2 One Act, three start dates 4 min

Say: The government commenced this in three stages. Left is in force now. Middle is November, and it is two provisions about Consent Managers. Right is May 2027, and it is everything you would recognise as compliance.

Do: Step through the three columns. This is the anchor image for the whole session, so let them read it before talking over it. If they take one picture away, it should be this one.

0:07 Slide 3 What changes on 13 November 2026 3 min

Say: Two provisions switch on. Consent Manager duties, and the Board's power to register them. That is the complete list.

0:10 Slide 4 Who that date is for 3 min

Say: Becoming a Consent Manager takes an Indian company, two crore of net worth and a certified platform. Unless someone here is planning that, this date carries no obligation for them.

0:13 Slide 5 Does anything get enforced on that date? 4 min

Say: Enforcement needs three things: an appointed Board, a power to inquire, a power to fine. All three arrive in May 2027. So the date to prepare against is May, not November.

Expect: This is where someone says they read that penalties start in November. Answer from the schedule, do not argue with the source.

0:17 Questions on timing · 3 min

Block 2 · Where your data is · 0:20 to 0:52

0:20 Slide 6 Every example today runs on one company 3 min

Say: Introduce SkillSetu. 400,000 learners, 60,000 under 18, twelve systems, three with no owner, one processor abroad with no contract. Invented company, real findings.

Ask: Which of those four numbers could you produce for your own business by Friday? Do not take answers, just let it sit.

0:23 Slides 7 to 9 The estate, one honest row, the children number 10 min

Say: Build the estate map. Then show one filled row, including the one that reads unknown three times. Then the 60,000 under 18 as 15 percent of the dots.

Land this: unknown is a finding, not a failure. Almost nobody can name their own under-18 count.

0:33 Workbook Section 1 · Data inventory 14 min

Say: Move to your workbook tab, section one. Fourteen minutes. Breadth before polish: every place personal data sits, including the ones that are not systems. Write unknown where you do not know.

Do: Stay off the microphone. At the halfway mark ask people to put a number in chat for how many rows they have, which is the only signal you get that the room is working.

Block 3 · What you have to tell people · 0:52 to 1:24

0:52 Slide 10 What a notice has to contain 4 min

Say: Five requirements from Rule 3, against what published notices usually do. Itemised is the word that does the work, and it is the one most notices fail.

0:56 Workbook Section 2 · Notice checklist 12 min

Say: Score your own notice against each line. You need the URL open in another tab. Where you cannot find something, that is the answer.

1:08 Slide 11 Data you collected before any of this commenced 4 min

Say: Three defensible positions per dataset: re-consent, a legitimate use under s.7, or delete. There is no fourth option called leave it and hope. The register matters more than the decision.

1:12 Workbook Section 3 · Legacy data position 10 min

Say: One line per dataset, a decision and the reasoning. A decision you can show reasoning for is defensible. No record is not.

Block 4 · When someone asks for their data · 1:34 to 1:53

1:34 Slide 12 Four requests, and what the clock actually says 4 min

Say: No statutory response period exists in this Act. The thirty days people repeat is not in the instrument. What does bind you is the period you publish yourself, capped at ninety days.

Slow down on: the erasure row, where a retention floor can oblige you to keep a record you have been asked to delete.

1:38 Workbook Section 4 · Rights request SOP 12 min

Say: One named owner, one inbox, one path. The point is that someone can run it while you are on leave.

Block 5 · When it goes wrong · 1:53 to 2:27

1:53 Slides 13 and 14 Two laws, one incident · Four clocks 7 min

Say: The six hours everyone quotes is CERT-In, a separate law, and it is live today. The DPDP clocks arrive in May 2027. Only one of the four faces is moving.

2:27 Slide 16 The next ninety days 4 min

Two things to watch on a webinar

There is no facilitator view, by design: nothing anyone types reaches us, so we cannot see who is working. Chat is the only signal, so ask for a number in chat at the midpoint of every working section.

Two hours fifty is long for a call. If attendance is thin after the break, the sections to compress are 6 and 7, because both can be finished afterwards from the workbook without having sat through the exercise.
