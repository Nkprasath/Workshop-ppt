## How to read this file

Every fact has an ID. Anything used in a slide, in the workbook, in the chat FAQ or in an
email cites its ID, so a claim can be traced back to a section or rule number in one step.

Confidence values used in the tables:

Verified means I read it in the Act, the Rules or the CERT-In direction and quoted it.
Verified with caveat means the text supports it but the phrasing in circulation overstates
or narrows it, and the caveat is written out in full.
Reported means it comes from a secondary source such as a law firm note or a news report,
sourced and dated, and it may be used only where the asset says who reported it.
Unverified means I could not source it to the Act, the Rules, a gazette notification, a PIB
release or a MeitY document. Unverified facts do not go into any attendee-facing material.
Contradicted means a claim circulating in the market is inconsistent with the primary text.
Those are useful, but only in Block 1 where we quote them and name the source.

### Delivery note on section 2A, which is not a fact

F130 is a construction of the notified text, not a fact in the sense the rest of this file
uses the word. The commencement notification and the sections it names are facts. The
conclusion that no penalty can be imposed on 13 November 2026 is a reading of how those
provisions interact, and a competent lawyer could in principle argue the other way, which is
why F131 sets out the contrary reading rather than pretending it does not exist.

This matters for delivery, because Blocks 1 and 2 together contradict published commentary
twice inside 55 minutes. If someone in the room pushes back, "we read the notification" is a
weak answer from a founder. A named lawyer's reading with the dependency chain behind it is
a strong one.

So Olivia has to own this reading before it is said on stage. Her name goes on the slide that
carries F130, and she takes the question if one comes, even though Prasath is presenting the
block. Walk her through F123 to F131 and F148 well before the day, and ask her directly
whether she will stand behind the conclusion in front of 75 people.

If she is not comfortable with it, the block softens and does not break. The softened version
presents what the notification lists, F123 and F124, then what commentators infer from it,
F108, then the dependency chain as an open question rather than a settled conclusion, and
lets the room draw its own conclusion. That version is weaker and it is still the most
accurate thing anyone in that room will have heard on the subject. Decide which version is
running before the slides are designed, because the two need different wording on two slides.
